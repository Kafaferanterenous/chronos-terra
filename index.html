What am I looking at?

Chronos-Terra is a tool for the curious. It exists to explore one question: do cosmic forces — solar storms, geomagnetic upheavals, the pull of the Moon and Sun — influence when and where earthquakes happen?

If we find a correlation

That's good. It could point toward new understanding.

If we don't find a correlation

That's good too. Knowing what doesn't matter is just as valuable — it narrows the search.

Either way

This is an observatory you can hold in your hands. It overlays 56 years of global earthquakes (USGS archive, M4.5+, 1970 → today) against solar-terrestrial drivers.

The map

Orange→red dots: historical quakes inside the timeline window at the bottom — bigger and redder = stronger. Cyan dots: quakes in the last 24 hours. Purple/blue/other colored dots: active GDACS hazards (cyclones, floods, wildfires, volcanoes, droughts). Blue lines: tectonic plate boundaries — most quakes hug them. Tiny gold dots (enable in ⚙️ Settings): world capital cities, click one for details.

The timeline

Drag the histogram to sweep through history; ▶ plays an animated sweep. The orange line above the histogram is the sunspot cycle for context. The ● Live button jumps to the past 7 days and watches for new events.

Correlation lab

Compare monthly series (sunspots, Kp geomagnetic index, F10.7 flux vs quake counts/energy/M6+) with lags from −24 to +36 months. Permutation p-values tell you if a peak beats random chance. Phase histograms test M6+ clustering against the 27-day Bartels solar rotation and 29.53-day spring/neap tide cycles.

Data sources

USGS earthquake archives & live feeds · GFZ Potsdam Kp/ap/SN/F10.7 since 1932 (Matzka et al. 2021) · SILSO sunspot numbers · NASA DONKI CME database · GDACS hazard alerts · Natural Earth / fraxen plate geometry · capitals reference values are approximations.

FAQ

Does a correlation prove cause?

No. Every number here is exploratory and uncorrected for all the ways the data can line up by chance. A peak is a question to investigate, never a mechanism.

Why does the data badge say “stale”?

The bundled OMNI2 daily series end 2025-12-31, and NASA's OMNIWeb archive itself usually publishes 1–2 weeks behind real time. Live feeds refresh from the badge; the slow OMNI series have their own “Refresh all stale” button in Graphs → data ▾, which extends them to NASA's latest published day rather than literally today.

Are the fields under a quake measured at the epicentre?

No. Bz, Dst, pressure, density and E-field are global L1 / planetary daily proxies sampled at the event time, as labelled in the popup.

Where are cached files written?

Beside the portable exe when that folder is writable, otherwise in your per-user app-data folder. The active paths are shown immediately below and in Settings.

What does the density heatmap show?

A kernel-density glow of the quakes currently drawn inside the visible timeline window. It is decimated during playback to keep the globe responsive; it is not a full-catalogue density map.

How do I export my results?

Graphs has ⤓ csv for the visible series and the finding export. The correlation lab has ⤓ csv for its lag scan and trigger-window profile. Settings offers a full-archive CSV.

❓ Help — how to use Chronos-Terra & glossary

1 · Core idea

Chronos-Terra overlays 56 years of global earthquakes (USGS archive, M4.5+, 1970 → today) against solar-terrestrial drivers — solar wind, geomagnetic indices, sunspot cycles — so you can explore whether cosmic forces influence when and where quakes happen. Finding a correlation is good; finding none is equally valuable. This is an exploratory research tool, not peer-reviewed science or a prediction.

2 · The map views (🌐 Globe · 🗺️ Flat)

  • Dots on the map are earthquakes inside the current timeline window: bigger and redder = stronger. Orange→red = older, cyan = last 24 h.
  • Blue lines — tectonic plate boundaries (most quakes hug them). Red triangles — volcanoes (GVP catalogue).
  • Coloured blobs — live GDACS hazard alerts (cyclone 🌀 · flood 🌊 · wildfire 🔥 · volcano 🌋 · drought 🌾).
  • Gold dots — world capital cities (turn on in the ⚙️ layers). Click any map feature for details.
  • Pulsing features = forecast; muted teal = known impact. Darken with the sidebar opacity slider.

3 · The timeline bar (bottom of map)

  • Drag the histogram to sweep through history; ▶ animates the sweep. The orange line/strip is the 11-year sunspot cycle (toggle ☀ cycle / ☀ yearly).
  • Range buttons 1D·7D·3M·1Y·5Y·10Y·ALL zoom the window. ● Live jumps to the last 24 h and watches for new events.
  • Live layers (cyan quakes, GDACS hazards) hide while the timeline plays.

4 · Graphs view (📈 Graphs)

  • Switch to Graphs and the map controls disappear — the chart takes the full window.
  • Check the series you want (eruptions, sunspots, Kp, F10.7 flux, quakes, proton density, SW pressure, IMF Bz, IMF |B|, Dst, SW E-field). Each is scaled independently so shapes compare, not magnitudes; the right edge shows each series' min/max.
  • Range selector picks the time window; ‹ › pager steps it backward/forward; M≥ sets the smallest magnitude counted in the quakes series.
  • Hover the chart: a dashed crosshair plus a floating tag shows the full date and every series value at that instant.
  • data ▾ manages the bundled NASA OMNIWeb (OMNI2) solar-wind series — each can be refreshed to NASA's latest published day (OMNIWeb trails real time), saved locally.
  • Below the chart: correlation stats, lag-strips (gold outline = strongest lag), and volcano/quarry context. If a line stops before "today", its data simply ends there — hover the tag to see the last date.

5 · Studies & news (📚 Studies)

  • Left: short summaries of peer-reviewed studies on solar-terrestrial influence, with links to the originals.
  • Right: a live news column — sun & space weather, volcanoes, extreme weather and today's quakes. Click any headline to open it. ⟳ refreshes manually; auto-refresh every 15 min.

6 · Moon & tides (🌙)

  • 3-D Earth–Moon scene of the gravitational tide. The gold ☾ marks the sub-lunar point — where the Moon is directly overhead — and teal ☀ the Sun's. The equatorial bulge + floating arrows show the tide-generating flow (equilibrium tide, exaggerated by the slider; real sizes are in cm in the readout).
  • The scene is driven by the timeline: press ▶ (or drag the histogram) and the sub-lunar point sweeps the whole planet once per ~24.8 h — that sweep is Earth's rotation. The day side stays put; the Moon travels over it.
  • Controls: drag the globe to spin it (same feel as the map view), scroll to zoom, double-click to zoom in. Press ▶ and pick a speed in the bar — from "30 m/s" (a slow crawl) up to "1 d/s" — to watch the Moon's overhead point sweep the planet. Playing wraps around: when it reaches the end of the archive it loops back instead of stopping.
  • Key dots: gold/teal = sub-points · orange→red = quakes in window · red = eruptions (GVP). Quakes & eruptions that happened within ~±3 days of a new moon — when the Sun and Moon sat on the same side of Earth and their pull added up — are highlighted bright gold. Toggle this off in Settings if you want plain colors.
  • Honest note: orbits and bulge are not to scale; the static equilibrium tide (~0.5 m lunar + ~0.25 m solar) is what the physics really says.

7 · Correlation lab (📊)

  • Pick a driver A (solar/space variable) and response B (quakes/volcanoes), set a lag of B behind A, then compute r. Positive lag = B happens AFTER A.
  • Permutation p-values tell you whether a peak beats random chance. Phase histograms test clustering against the 27-day solar rotation and 29.53-day lunar spring/neap cycle.
  • Trigger windows average the quake response around event days (new moon, full moon, Kp storm, F10.7 high, X-flares, Earth-directed CMEs, M6+ quakes, climate events) — pick a ±-day window and search.
  • Time-series overlays add a lag-scan strip under the graphs. The dashed band is an uncorrected 95% null; the strip also reports Benjamini–Hochberg q-values across every scanned lag, and A/B previous window ghosts the immediately preceding equal-width window so you can see whether a peak is stable.
  • Region restricts the quake series, lag scan and event markers to a coarse latitude/longitude box. These are rectangles, not tectonic-plate boundaries.

8 · Settings, shortcuts & tips

  • ⚙️ Settings: spoken announcements, checking-for-new-events interval, timeline sweep speed, DONKI history depth.
  • F11 fullscreen · Ctrl/Cmd + / − / 0 zoom in / out / reset · Esc close overlays/help/tutorial. Window bounds and zoom are remembered between runs.
  • The data badge in the top bar opens a health popup: per-feed status, stale bundled OMNI series, a one-click refresh for live feeds/archives, and the exact cache paths. Slow OMNI series have their own “Refresh all stale” button in the Graphs → data dialog. OMNI2 is curated and may stop 1–2 weeks short of today; the refresh follows NASA's published cap.
  • Enable Density heatmap under Layers for a glow of the quakes currently drawn in the window. A small spinner beside each panel title shows when live feeds, graph series or trigger shuffles are still running.
  • Exports: ⤓ csv in Graphs covers the visible series, ⤓ csv in the Correlation lab covers the lag scan and trigger profile, and Settings can export the entire archive. The ℹ️ panel now includes a short FAQ.
  • ℹ️ What am I looking at? gives the quick orientation · ❓ this help screen is always one click away · ▶ tour replays the intro.
  • Heads-up: the proton-density → quake idea is the Marchitelli et al. (2020) hypothesis — exploratory only.

9 · Glossary — every acronym

TermMeaning
ACENASA's Advanced Composition Explorer — spacecraft at the L1 point measuring the live solar wind (SWEPAM instrument).
ApLinear (amplitude) version of Kp; a bigger-number daily geomagnetic activity file index (GFZ Potsdam).
Bz (IMF Bz)The north/south component of the interplanetary magnetic field. Strongly southward Bz opens Earth's magnetosphere and feeds storms.
CMECoronal Mass Ejection — a billion-ton cloud of solar plasma/magnetic field hurled into space; the main driver of geomagnetic storms.
DONKINASA's Database of Notifications, Knowledge & Information — the CME / flare catalogue used here.
DstDisturbance storm-time index (nT) — measures the strength of the equatorial ring current; strongly negative = magnetic storm.
E-field / SW E-fieldSolar-wind motional electric field V×Bz (mV/m) — the rate energy feeds into the magnetosphere.
EUVExtreme UltraViolet — solar radiation that heats/ionizes Earth's upper atmosphere.
F10.7Solar radio flux at 10.7 cm (in sfu) — a centuries-spanning proxy for the Sun's overall activity.
GDACSGlobal Disaster Alert and Coordination System — live alerts for cyclones, floods, wildfires, volcanoes, droughts.
GFZ (Potsdam)German Research Centre for Geosciences — source of the Kp/ap/SN/F10.7 series since 1932 (Matzka et al. 2021).
GSMGeocentric Solar-Magnetospheric coordinate frame — the reference for Bz used in geomagnetic-coupling physics.
GVPSmithsonian Global Volcanism Program — the eruption catalogue behind the volcano layer & series.
IMFInterplanetary Magnetic Field — the Sun's magnetic field carried by the solar wind; described by Bz (GSM) and |B| (total strength).
ISCInternational Seismological Centre — cross-referenced quake catalogue behind the graph series.
KpPlanetary geomagnetic index (0–9) — how agitated Earth's magnetic field is; proxies solar-wind pressure on the magnetosphere.
L1Lagrange point 1 — the Sun–Earth balance point ~1.5 million km sunward, where ACE/SOHO sit ~1 h ahead of the solar wind's arrival.
M2.5 / M4.5 / M5.6 / M6+Earthquake magnitudes: M4.5+ is the USGS archive floor used here; M5.6+ is the threshold in the Marchitelli proton-density hypothesis; M6+ is the "strong" tier in the correlation lab.
NASAUS National Aeronautics and Space Administration — DONKI, ACE, OMNIWeb data.
NOAAUS National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration — SWPC and the live SWEPAM proton feed.
OMNI2 / OMNIWebNASA/GSFC's consolidated near-Earth solar-wind dataset (OMNI2 = daily, OMNIWeb = fine resolution) — the historical magnetosphere series used by the Graphs view.
sfuSolar Flux Unit (10⁻²² W m⁻² Hz⁻¹) — the unit of the F10.7 index.
SILSO / SNSILSO = World Data Center Sunspot Index and Long-term Solar Observations; SN = daily/monthly sunspot number (the 11-year cycle).
SOHOESA/NASA Solar and Heliospheric Observatory — sits at L1 as the reference point for the live proton "next to Earth" framing.
SWEPAMSolar Wind Electron, Proton and Alpha Monitor on ACE — the instrument behind the live proton-density feed.
SWPCNOAA Space Weather Prediction Center — US official alerts & particle feeds.
USGSUS Geological Survey — the earthquake archive and live feeds used throughout.
UTCCoordinated Universal Time — the time standard all timestamps in this app are stored in.
V×BSolar-wind velocity cross magnetic-field — the coupling that creates the motional E-field feeding the magnetosphere.
